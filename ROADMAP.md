NetLabCraft aims at Cisco Packet Tracer parity for teaching and lab work, and at being better
in places: real frames you can open in any packet analyser, an open lab format, agent control
(MCP and CLI), and the browser. This page is the summary. The detail is in:

- [`docs/target-app-parity.md`](docs/target-app-parity.md): the authoritative assessment, method and weights
- [`docs/gaps.md`](docs/gaps.md): every known shortfall, ranked; the work list
- [`docs/roadmap.md`](docs/roadmap.md): milestones, current focus, alpha and beta gates
- [`docs/labs/`](docs/labs/README.md): the CCNA lab book, 49 labs (36 topic labs, 11 troubleshooting labs and 2 inspection labs) that run end to end as written
- [`docs/command-reference.md`](docs/command-reference.md): generated by `cargo xtask parity` (engine commands and console grammar)
- Area checklists: [protocols and commands](docs/protocol-parity.md) · [UI](docs/ui-parity.md) ·
  [hardware](docs/hardware-parity.md) · [localization](docs/localization-parity.md)
- [`docs/architecture.md`](docs/architecture.md): how it is built

## Progress log

- **2026-11-18 — A grid for each level of the physical workspace.** A four-squares button on the physical bar (also View ▸ Grid Preferences… and the place menu) opens Grid Preferences: per level (Intercity, City, Building, Generic Container) a box that turns the grid on and Grid-X and Grid-Y spacings in metres, and a line colour from a palette or red, green and blue. The grid is drawn thin under the cables and devices with its spacing at the corner, Align physical workspace objects snaps to it, and Shift+G toggles it for the level shown. Kept in the preferences file. ([`ui-parity.md`](docs/ui-parity.md))
- **2026-11-18 — Workspace hardening.** Hiding the Physical tab in Preferences also hides the Logical and Physical switch of the workspace bar. A remembered native device window opens on the screen it is on now (a monitor that was unplugged no longer leaves it out of reach) and the state a native window keeps is dropped with it. In the wiring closet every port is a keyboard node: Tab to a port, Enter to start a cable, Tab to another, Enter to finish it. One row ("Port, {}") added to each of the six catalogs.
- **2026-11-18 — Proximity, second round.** A Bluetooth speaker pairs with a music player only when their four-digit pairing codes match and the speaker takes music (a headset pairs with no player), an RFID card can have an id typed in (up to eight hex digits), a trip sensor can report a crossing of its 50 m wire (at a direction you set) instead of anything being near, a beacon keeps a log of the things that came into its range and left it (`iot.beacon`), a smart LED's brightness sets how much light it gives the room, and speakers, players, buzzers, alarms and sirens play the pitch they are given while a sound detector can be tuned to hear only that pitch.
- **2026-11-18 — Resize Shape is a tool.** The second toolbar's Resize Shape button was greyed out although resizing worked through Select. It is now a tool of its own, picked by the button or Alt+R (the 88th row of the shortcut table, counted from `TABLE`): click a rectangle, ellipse or line, drag its handles (`note.resize`, one undo step per drag); devices are not picked or moved, Esc returns to Select, and it is disabled in the physical view, which has no drawings.
- **2026-11-18 — Each place has its own physics.** With `environment.physics` (a checkbox on the Environments window's Advanced tab) the heaters, air conditioners, fans, furnaces, humidifiers, sprinklers, fire sprinklers, windows and lamps change the variables of the place they stand in, not only the smart room: a part moves a variable toward where it settles at a rate (minutes, not instantly), the place settles back when it stops, flames make smoke, carbon monoxide and heat, an open window pulls the place toward the one above it (faster in wind), and a sound fades with the distance to the sensor. Off by default: older labs behave as before. See [decisions.md](docs/decisions.md).
- **2026-11-18 — Pictures in the lab: Set Background Image.** Every place (the Intercity map, a city, a building, a wiring closet) and the logical workspace can have a background picture (original size, tiled, stretched to the map area, custom width or height), set from a new picture button on the workspace bar, Shift+I, View ▸ Set Background Image or the menu of a place. A place can also wear a picture as its icon on the map above it, and its size in metres gives its map a scale that cable lengths, cell tower reach and Wi-Fi range use, so the map of a real city can be scaled. Pictures (PNG and JPEG, checked, stripped of metadata and scaled down to 4096 pixels and 8 MiB) are stored once each inside the lab file (file version 12); Preferences ▸ Image Cleanup lists them and removes them. 11 new engine commands.
- **2026-11-18 — Cables are objects.** A cable in the logical workspace lights up under the pointer, a click with the Select tool picks it, the Delete tool and the Delete key remove it, and a right-click offers Properties…, Open First Device, Open Second Device, Cable Length… and Delete (Show Info too in the physical workspace and the wiring closet). The Cable Properties window shows the kind, both ends, the length, whether it is up and why it is down (a switched-off device, a shut down port, a serial clock or framing problem, ports fixed at different speeds, the wrong cable, a run that is too long), the transceivers of a fiber cable and buttons to set both ends to single-mode or multi-mode. A cable's accessible node opens the window on Enter. One new engine command, `link.info`; 27 rows added to each of the six catalogs.
- **2026-11-18 — Python and JavaScript on boards.** A Board SBC now runs a documented subset of Python and a Board MCU a subset of JavaScript, besides BoardScript: variables, numbers, text, lists, `if`, `while`, `for`, functions, `print` or `console.log`, `time.sleep` or `delay` in simulated time, a `loop()` function called again and again, and the board's pins as `digitalRead`, `digitalWrite`, `analogRead`, `analogWrite`, `customRead`, `customWrite`, `rgb` and `channel`. A Language list on the Programming tab picks it, the lab file keeps it, every program is bounded (steps, calls, memory, text) and errors show with their line. See [board-languages.md](docs/board-languages.md); labs 73 and 74.
- **2026-11-18 — Real connectors in the device windows.** The Physical tab of every device now draws each port as the connector it really is, the same ones as the wiring closet but larger and more detailed: RJ45 jacks with their latch and link and activity lights, SFP cages with a labelled tab, serial D-shells, coaxial and phone jacks, a radio mark for Wi-Fi and cellular, and pin-header sockets on the boards, on a front panel with the model's name, screws, vents and power light. Ports can be pointed at, clicked or reached with the keyboard to see what is cabled to them.
- **2026-11-17 — Release 0.8.0.** The eighth release: pointer input like the reference app (rubber-band selection with the left button, panning with the right or middle button, a single click to enter a place), a wiring closet you can read and zoom with real connectors, name plates and port blocks, redrawn device pictures, Save as PDF and a Print window, every Preferences tab (an administrative lock and user folder, Show/Hide, Font and colours, Miscellaneous backups and buffer actions, the Answer Tree filter), and 46 more models (146 in all): eight Smart Home things, twelve Smart City and Industrial things and 26 actuators and sensors for boards, with models shown in more than one group, still unsigned and with FreeBSD packages built in CI.
- **2026-11-18 — OSPF: a copy of every LSA on every router.** `show ip ospf database` shows the router's own copy of each LSA: a copy is one second older per adjacency it crossed, a new instance reaches each router a flooding hop (10 ms) after its originator, and an LSA the originator withdraws is held at MaxAge (age 3600) in the other routers for five seconds before every router removes it; `show ip ospf maxage-database` lists those. `show ip ospf` counts the SPF runs of the router (its database changed) and shows `timers throttle spf START HOLD MAX` (default 50, 200 and 5000 ms), `clear ip ospf process` starts the count over. New `debug ip ospf lsa-generation` (LSAs originated and flushed), `flood` (LSAs received and sent) and `packet` (every OSPF packet heard), all switched off by `undebug all`. Three grammar entries added; the copies are worked out from the converged state, not kept ([gaps.md](docs/gaps.md) item 1).
- **2026-11-18 — OSPF authentication: key rollover, key chains and a replay check.** An interface that holds several `ip ospf message-digest-key` lines signs one copy of each packet per key and accepts any key it holds, so a rollover (new key on one router, then the other, then the old key off) keeps the adjacency. `key chain NAME` with `key`, `key-string`, `accept-lifetime` and `send-lifetime` (read against the device's clock, which `clock set` moves) feeds `ip ospf authentication key-chain NAME`; `show key chain` says which keys are valid now and which one signs. A message-digest packet whose sequence number is below the last one heard from that sender under that key is dropped and counted. 14 new grammar entries, 2 new modes. The OSPF state is worked out from the configuration alone, so it reads a chain at the time of the last `clock set` ([gaps.md](docs/gaps.md) item 1).
- **2026-11-18 — Lab 49: OSPF authentication.** Rolls an MD5 key over without losing the adjacency, moves the keys into a key chain whose send lifetime starts in 2030 (the simulated clock is in 2026, so nothing signs until `clock set` moves it), and protects OSPFv3 with an AH header. Lab numbers 49-52 were reserved for the OSPF work.
- **2026-11-18 — OSPFv3 authentication with a real AH header.** `ospfv3 authentication ipsec spi N md5|sha1 KEY` on an interface (or `null` to opt out) and `area N authentication ipsec spi …` under `ipv6 router ospf` wrap every OSPFv3 hello in an IPsec Authentication Header over IPv6 (RFC 4302: the hop limit, traffic class and flow label count as zero, the addresses are covered; keys are 32 hexadecimal digits for md5 and 40 for sha1), and two routers become neighbours only when the SPI, the algorithm and the key agree. `show ipv6 ospf neighbor` lists the routers refused and why, `show ipv6 ospf` the protection of each interface. 6 new grammar entries. Left in [gaps.md](docs/gaps.md) item 1: the other packet types, ESP encryption, type 7 keys, rollover.
- **2026-11-17 — Twelve more Smart City and Industrial things.** An atmospheric pressure monitor, a wind detector, a carbon dioxide detector and a fire monitor read the room (or the variable a place sets), a fire sprinkler sprays by itself while the room is in flames and puts them out, an old car adds carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide while its engine runs, and a beacon, an RFID card with its reader, a trip sensor and a smart LED (brightness and colour) round out the group, and an industrial signal generator (a level and a frequency other things read) joins the Industrial group: 146 models. The proximity things use positions in the workspace with fixed ranges in metres (RFID 30 m, trip sensor 50 m, Bluetooth 60 m, beacon 100 m; 1.8 workspace units to the metre, or the same city on a layout). The device box lists a model in more than one subgroup where the reference does: the Smart LED in Smart City, Industrial and Actuators, and the Smart Sensor, Humidity Monitor and CO Detector in Home and in Smart City. Rows in the six catalogs. Shallow in places: [gaps.md](docs/gaps.md) item 16.
- **2026-11-17 — The rest of the Preferences tabs.** Administrative keeps a local password (a salted PBKDF2 hash, never the password) that locks the Interface, Show/Hide, Custom Interfaces and Miscellaneous tabs read-only until it is entered, and a user folder for the Open and Save dialogs and automatic backups. Show/Hide hides device window tabs (Physical, Config, CLI, router and switch Config and CLI, Services, Desktop, phone and smart-thing screens, Attributes), wireless and cellular pages and the coverage field, and turns the wireless boundary and colour fade of View Wireless Signals on or off. Font sets sizes in points (8 pt is 100%), the CLI font and size, the tooltip size and a named colour table for the consoles and tooltips. Miscellaneous writes automatic backups every 1 to 30 minutes (never over a file), decides what happens when the event list is full, buffers only the events the filter lets through and shows a device window taskbar. Answer Tree filters the Assessment Tree by device kind. Custom Interfaces and Publishers say they have nothing to set, and Image Cleanup shows an empty table. Two new engine commands (`file.backup`, `sim.prune_events`), 125 rows added to each of the six catalogs; what stays out on purpose is in `docs/gaps.md` item 12.
- **2026-11-17 — Eight more Smart Home things.** An appliance, a Bluetooth speaker, a ceiling fan, a furnace, a humidifier, a humiture monitor, a portable music player and a sound frequency detector join the Smart Home group (134 models). They act on the room through the same physics as the heater and the sprinkler: a furnace heats faster than a heater, a ceiling fan brings the room toward the outside temperature, a humidifier raises the humidity, and a music player plays a tone that a sound detector reads (pitch and volume); a Bluetooth speaker pairs by distance with the nearest music player within 60 m. A sensor can now report a second reading (the humiture monitor's humidity), the room gains carbon dioxide, flames, sound, pressure and wind, and the sensors that read them are in the next pull requests. A request path with a space no longer cuts a two-word sensor's reading off. Rows in the six catalogs. Shallow in places: [gaps.md](docs/gaps.md) items 14 and 16.
- **2026-11-17 — Twenty-six more components.** The Actuators group gains an Air Cooler, an Alarm, a Ceiling and a Floor Sprinkler, a Dimmable LED, a Heating Element, an RGB LED, a Servo and a Speaker (the Buzzer is listed as the Piezo Speaker); the Sensors group gains a Flex Sensor, a Generic Environment Sensor, a Generic Sensor, a Humidity, a Humiture, a Smoke, a Temperature, a Motion, a Metal and a Wind Sensor, a Membrane Potentiometer, three switches (push button toggle, rocker, toggle push button), a Trip Wire, a Water Detector and a Water Sensor (126 models in all, each with a picture drawn in code and rows in the six catalogs). They wire to a board's pins with the IoT custom cable like the first components: BoardScript writes a dimmable LED's brightness, a servo's angle and an RGB LED's colour (`rgb(R, G, B)`) and reads a humiture sensor's second value with `channel(PIN, 1)`.

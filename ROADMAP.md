# NetLabCraft roadmap

**Stage: pre-alpha** · every alpha and beta gate is met, packages are built by `release.yml` (not code-signed yet); the owner decides when the stage label changes

> **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-13 · **Last updated:** 2026-11-13 · **Change:** minor (M9.5: IKEv1 group 24 is a real Diffie-Hellman group) · also merged with M9.6 and M9.8 (results replay; Resize Shape and accessible devices) · earlier: minor (M9.5: AH next to ESP in one transform set is a real bundle), minor (M9.5: the IKE SA has a lifetime and is replaced before it runs out), minor (seven troubleshooting labs and the console views they turned up) · also merged with M9.2 and M9.3 (routing timers; real DES, 3DES, AH and elliptic-curve Diffie-Hellman), minor (releases and packaging), major (the CCNA lab book, security leftovers, instructor tools, Simulation-mode and wireless leftovers, end devices, automation depth and routing leftovers landed; numbers measured again, readiness re-estimated, gaps re-ranked) · **Target:** Cisco Packet Tracer 9.0 (as used in CCNA courses)

NetLabCraft aims at Cisco Packet Tracer parity for teaching and lab work, and at being better
in places: real frames you can open in any packet analyser, an open lab format, agent control
(MCP and CLI), and the browser. This page is the summary. The detail is in:

- [`docs/target-app-parity.md`](docs/target-app-parity.md): the authoritative assessment, method and weights
- [`docs/gaps.md`](docs/gaps.md): every known shortfall, ranked; the work list
- [`docs/roadmap.md`](docs/roadmap.md): milestones, current focus, alpha and beta gates
- [`docs/labs/`](docs/labs/README.md): the CCNA lab book, 27 labs (20 topic labs and 7 troubleshooting labs) that run end to end as written
- [`docs/command-reference.md`](docs/command-reference.md): generated by `cargo xtask parity` (engine commands and console grammar)
- Area checklists: [protocols and commands](docs/protocol-parity.md) · [UI](docs/ui-parity.md) ·
  [hardware](docs/hardware-parity.md) · [localization](docs/localization-parity.md)
- [`docs/architecture.md`](docs/architecture.md): how it is built

## Progress log

- **2026-11-13 — BoardScript messages in Spanish.** Every BoardScript compile error ("line 3: `end` without an `if` or `while`") and run-time stop ("stopped: line 2: the board has no pin D9") now reads in the interface language: the status bar, the Programming tab's message and its output show them in Spanish when the interface is Spanish. Nothing decides on the English text (the state, the line and the pins are typed fields); the engine, the console and MCP keep the English message. What a program prints is shown as it was written.
- **2026-11-13 — The smart things share a room with physics.** `iot.environment` switches a small deterministic room on: its temperature, humidity, soil moisture, smoke, CO and light move in simulated time as the smart devices work (a heater warms 1.2 degrees a minute up to 40, an air conditioner cools 1.5 down to 10, an open window or garage couples the room to the outside and lets daylight in, a sprinkler wets the air and the soil, a fan clears smoke and CO, a lamp lights the room) and the thermostat, temperature, humidity, soil, smoke, CO and motion sensors read it, so a rule "if the temperature is below 19 switch the heater on" closes a loop in simulated time. Integer thousandths and one-second steps keep it identical on every platform. It is off by default (older labs keep their own readings), set per lab and saved with it (lab file version 2), shown and editable in the IoT service's page, and the new `iot-climate` sample runs the loop. Rooms and a day and night cycle are not modelled.
- **2026-11-13 — IKEv1 group 24.** The 2048-bit MODP group with a 256-bit prime-order subgroup (RFC 5114 §2.3) runs for real in `wire/src/dh.rs`: the prime, generator and subgroup order are the RFC's constants, checked against the RFC 5114 appendix A.3 test data (public values and shared secret), and a peer's value must be in range and in the subgroup (y^q = 1 mod p). `crypto isakmp policy` / `group 24` now does a real exchange, and `show crypto isakmp sa` no longer says stand-in. The only crypto stand-in left in IPsec is a transform set with no cipher.
- **2026-11-13 — AH bundled with ESP.** A transform set with `ah-*` and `esp-*` transforms now produces an AH SA and an ESP SA, each with its own SPIs and keys in both directions. IKEv1 quick mode proposes both protocols under one proposal number and derives KEYMAT per protocol; IKEv2 negotiates the AH child SA with a second CREATE_CHILD_SA right after IKE_AUTH (a Child SA has one protocol) and renews it after the ESP one. Packets leave with AH outside ESP (ESP in tunnel mode, then AH in transport mode over the ESP packet, RFC 4301 §4.5): the receiver checks and strips AH first and refuses plain ESP from a bundle peer; the event list shows `AH … wrapping ESP SPI …`, and `show crypto ipsec sa` shows both halves. The stand-in markers for this case are gone; IKEv1 group 24 is still open.
- **2026-11-13 — IKE SA lifetimes and rekeying.** The ISAKMP and IKE SAs now age in simulated time and are replaced before they run out. IKEv1: `lifetime SECONDS` under `crypto isakmp policy` (the shorter of the two ends applies); near the end the router that started the SA runs a new main mode next to the old one, and the new cookies and keys replace the old ones while the IPsec SAs carry on untouched. IKEv2: `lifetime SECONDS` in `crypto ikev2 profile`; the initiator sends CREATE_CHILD_SA with an IKE-protocol SA payload, a nonce and a new Diffie-Hellman value (RFC 7296 §1.3.2), the keys come from the old SK_d (§2.18), the child SAs move to the new IKE SA and the old one is deleted with an INFORMATIONAL exchange. `show crypto isakmp sa` and `show crypto ikev2 sa` show cookies or SPIs, age, time left and replacements. AH bundled with ESP and IKEv1 group 24 are still open.
- **2026-11-13 — Devices on the workspace are accessible.** A screen reader now finds every device on the canvas as its own node (`R1, Router 3GE`, with `, selected` when it is), Tab and the arrow keys move between devices in id order and select them, and Enter, Space or the reader's click opens the device like a mouse click. Cables are not nodes yet.
- **2026-11-13 — Resize Shape.** Click a rectangle, ellipse or line with the Select tool and drag its handles (eight on a box, one at each end of a line); a box dragged past its opposite side flips, sides stay between 4 and 100000 units, and a whole drag is one undo step. The same thing is the `note.resize` command for the CLI and MCP.
- **2026-11-13 — Results that resist forgery.** A JSON results file (version 2) now embeds the student's final network, and `activity.verify_results` runs the activity's checks again against it and compares the score, the points per item and which items passed with what the file claims, in addition to the signature and the same-activity check. A forged score fails unless the network really earns it. The result says `replayed` and `replay_matches` and lists what differed; a CSV or an older file is checked for hand edits only and says so, and time claims are not checked. The Activity window shows the three outcomes (replayed, not replayed, forged). A hostile or oversized embedded network is refused, never a crash.
- **2026-11-13 — Troubleshooting labs.** Seven new labs (21 to 27) in the lab book build a working network, break it with commands and walk the diagnosis: VLAN and trunk faults, static routes and host masks, OSPF neighbours that will not form, an ACL in the wrong place, DHCP relay and pool faults, NAT overload faults and the spanning-tree root. Walking them turned up console gaps that are now fixed: `show interfaces switchport` for every port and `show interfaces X trunk`, `no ip proxy-arp`, `show ip ospf neighbor` by router ID or interface, `show ip route NET MASK`, the ACL `log` keyword with log messages, `clear (ip) access-list counters NAME`, `show ip dhcp pool NAME` with the addresses left, NAT to an interface without `overload` serving one host at a time, NAT hits and misses and `clear ip nat statistics`, NAT rules counting matches on their ACL, `show spanning-tree root`, `bridge` and `blockedports`. The book's console numbers are measured again.
- **2026-11-13 — VRRPv3 router advertisements for IPv6.** The master of a VRRPv3 IPv6 group now announces the virtual router the way RFC 5798 asks: a router advertisement from the virtual link-local address with the virtual MAC when it takes the role and every 200 s, and it alone answers router solicitations (a backup stays quiet), so a host that autoconfigures takes the virtual address as its router and follows it when the master changes. The routing leftovers are done (GLBP hellos stay off the wire: the format is not public).
- **2026-11-13 — EIGRP for IPv6 timers.** `ipv6 hello-interval eigrp AS S` and `ipv6 hold-time eigrp AS S` on interfaces (in the running configuration): hellos go out at each interface's own interval announcing its hold time, a neighbour that falls silent keeps its routes until that hold time runs out and is then dropped (a syslog line, DUAL's queries and updates), and `show ipv6 eigrp interfaces` lists the timers and `neighbors` the silent ones.
- **2026-11-13 — RIPng timers.** RIPng now behaves like RIPv2 in simulated time: `timers basic UPDATE INVALID HOLDDOWN FLUSH` under `ipv6 router rip` (30 180 180 300 by default, in the running configuration), a silent neighbour's routes kept until the invalid time, then possibly down at metric 16 with a hold-down and a flush, and triggered responses a second after a change with withdrawn prefixes at metric 16; `show ipv6 rip` shows the timers and silent neighbours, `show ipv6 rip database` the possibly-down prefixes.
- **2026-11-13 — Real elliptic-curve Diffie-Hellman.** IKE groups 19 (P-256) and 20 (P-384) run RFC 5903 for real (`wire/src/ec.rs`, point multiplication over the project's own big integers, peer points checked), verified against the RFC's published exchanges; IKEv1 main mode and IKEv2 IKE_SA_INIT carry real x‖y public values and derive keys from the real shared secret. The IKE SA itself is still never rekeyed.
- **2026-11-13 — Real AH.** `wire/src/ah.rs` implements the Authentication Header of RFC 4302: the ICV covers the IPv4 header with its mutable fields zeroed, then the AH header and the payload, with HMAC-SHA1-96, HMAC-MD5-96 or HMAC-SHA-256-128. A transform set of `ah-sha-hmac`, `ah-md5-hmac` or `ah-sha256-hmac` now negotiates an AH SA (IKEv1 quick mode and IKEv2 child SA) and sends protocol 51 packets in tunnel mode that the far router verifies; captures and the event list show `AH SPI … ICV …`. AH next to ESP in one set, the elliptic-curve groups and IKE SA rekeying are still open.
- **2026-11-13 — Real DES and 3DES.** `wire/src/des.rs` implements DES (FIPS 46-3) and three-key 3DES (SP 800-67) with CBC, checked against published known-answer vectors; IKEv1 policies (`encryption des` and `3des`), IKEv2 proposals (3DES) and ESP (`esp-des`, `esp-3des`) now encrypt for real with 8-byte blocks and IVs, where a stand-in used to stand. AH, the elliptic-curve groups and IKE SA rekeying are still open.
- **2026-11-13 — One modular-exponentiation engine.** TLS and SSH's group 14 now run on the same Diffie-Hellman code as IKE (`wire/src/dh.rs`); the duplicate Montgomery arithmetic and the hard-coded prime in `wire/src/modp.rs` are gone.
- **2026-11-13 — Releases.** `release.yml` builds Linux (AppImage, deb, rpm, tar.gz; x86_64 and aarch64), Windows (MSI and portable zip), macOS (universal dmg and CLI zip) and the web build, with SHA256SUMS, and creates a draft GitHub Release; CI runs `cargo xtask ci` on every pull request; Dependabot, issue and pull request templates, `CONTRIBUTING.md` and `SECURITY.md` are in. Builds are not code-signed yet.
- **2026-10-31 — Switching and routing leftovers.** IP phones mark voice CoS 5 (EF) and signalling CoS 3, switches carry 802.1p priorities on tagged ports and map them with `mls qos map cos-dscp`, `mls qos cos` and `switchport priority extend cos`; VRRP advertisement timers, master-down interval and skew, `vrrp N track`, and VRRPv3 for IPv6; EIGRP for IPv6 sends its updates, queries and replies on the wire, with `variance` and `ipv6 summary-address eigrp` (and `ip summary-address eigrp` with a Null0 route); RIP timers with hold-down, triggered updates and poisoned reverse, and RIPng; VTP pruning.
- **2026-10-31 — Automation depth.** RESTCONF speaks XML as well as JSON (Accept and Content-Type) and IPv6 addresses and transport; NETCONF has a candidate datastore with commit, discard and validate, locks between sessions, and sessions kept open between commands; `https://` runs a real TLS 1.2 handshake and NETCONF runs over SSH 2.0; the controller discovers routers over CDP and LLDP from a seed, keeps an inventory with model and software, and pushes templates with per-router variables.
- **2026-10-31 — Phone settings and power supplies.** The IP phone's Settings screen (protocol, call server, extension, MAC, voice VLAN, TFTP server, DHCP or static address); routers and switches have one or two power-supply bays, drawn in the Physical tab, and taking out the last supply switches the device off.
- **2026-10-31 — Programmable boards.** Board SBC and Board MCU with digital and analog pins run BoardScript, a small language of our own (variables, conditions, loops, pins, sleep in simulated time, reports to the IoT server); IoT Custom cables join a pin to a smart thing; a Programming tab with editor, run, stop, pins, variables and output; board sample lab.

- **2026-10-30 — DHCP snooping and ARP inspection.** Untrusted ports drop DHCP server answers with a reason, trusted ones build the binding table, and ARP inspection checks ARP against it; rate limits err-disable a flooding port.
- **2026-10-30 — Routers as IPv6 clients.** `ipv6 address autoconfig [default]` and `ipv6 address dhcp` on router interfaces, asked for again after `no shutdown` and reloads.

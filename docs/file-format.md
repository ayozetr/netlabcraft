A lab is one UTF-8 JSON file (file version 12; versions 1 to 11 still open: version 2 added the lab's `environment`, version 3 the cellular radio below, version 4 the outline and fill of drawings, version 5 the firewall of end devices, version 6 the PPPoE account and service, version 7 the VPN Client and the remote-access server, version 8 NetFlow, version 9 the wiring closet's furniture, version 10 the fiber modes, USB and octal cables, version 11 the environments of the places, version 12 the pictures). It is the serde form of `netlabcraft_model::Topology`
(`crates/model/src/lib.rs`), wrapped with a format tag and version (`crates/model/src/file.rs`):

```json
{"format": "netlabcraft", "version": 12, "topology": {"devices": [...], "links": [...], "next_id": 7, "notes": "...", "annotations": [...]}}
```

Fields that hold their default value are left out when saving, so a small lab stays small and an
older file without a newer field still opens.

## Topology

| Field | Holds |
|---|---|
| `devices` | The devices (below). |
| `links` | Cables: `a` and `b`, each `{"device": id, "port": index}`, and `cable` (`straight-through`, `cross-over`, `fiber-single-mode`, `fiber-multi-mode` (an older `fiber` reads as multi-mode), `serial-dce`, `serial-dte`, `coaxial`, `phone`; left out when automatic). On a serial cable `a` is the DCE end. IoT custom cables are not here: they belong to the board (`board.wires`). |
| `physical` | Where the equipment stands; left out until the physical view is used. `places` (each `id`, `kind`: `city`, `building` or `closet`, `name`, `x`, `y`, `parent`), `homes` (a device's `place` and position), `lengths` (hand-set cable lengths: `a`, `b`, `meters`) and, from file version 9: `furniture` (each `id`, `place` (a closet), `kind`: `rack`, `shelf` or `table`, `name`, `x`, `y`, `size`: rack units 4 to 48 or shelf levels 1 to 8), `fittings` (passive devices: `id`, `place`, `kind`: `copper-patch-panel`, `fiber-patch-panel`, `copper-wall-mount`, `fiber-wall-mount` or `power-strip`, `name`, `ports` (ports or outlets), `on` (a power strip's switch)), `mounts` (what stands on furniture: `item` (`{"device": id}` or `{"fitting": id}`), `furniture`, `slot` (0-based rack unit or shelf level, from the top) and `units` (rack units taken)), `patch_wires` (`a` and `b`, each `{"panel": {"fitting", "port"}}` or `{"device": {"device", "port"}}`: a patch cord joins two panel ports, a drop joins a panel port to a device port) and `plugs` (a device in a power strip's outlet: `device`, `strip`, `outlet`). Loading drops what does not fit: furniture outside a closet, a mount that overlaps another, a plug or a wire that crosses closets, a wire end that is not a port, duplicate ids, and lists past their caps (200 pieces of furniture, 400 passive devices, 4000 wires). `scenes`: what each place shows, one entry per place (`place`: its number, 0 for the Intercity map; `background`: as the lab's `background`, laid over the map inside the place; `icon`: an id from `images`, worn instead of the drawn icon on the map above; `size`: `width`, `length`, `height` in metres and `scale`, metres per workspace unit, which replaces the built-in 1 m (city) or 100 m (Intercity) per unit when cable lengths, cell tower reach and Wi-Fi range are worked out). Loading drops entries of places that are gone, pictures that are not there and sizes that are not positive finite numbers. |
| `images` | The lab's pictures, left out when there are none: an object from the id of a picture (the SHA-1 of its bytes, 40 lower-case hexadecimal digits, so one picture used twice is stored once) to `{"kind": "png" or "jpeg", "w", "h", "data"}` (`data` is the file, base64). Added by the picture commands, which check, strip and scale pictures first: at most 4096 pixels on a side and 8 MiB each, 64 pictures and 32 MiB in all. Loading drops a picture that breaks a limit, whose bytes are not what its `kind` says or not base64, or whose key is not the hash of its bytes, and what pointed at it. The file never names a path. Pictures nothing uses are dropped on load and by every command that stops using one. |
| `background` | The background of the logical workspace, left out when none: `image` (an id from `images`), `mode` (`original`, `tiled`, `fit`, `width` or `height`; `original` when left out) and `custom` (the width or height in workspace units for the last two). |
| `consoles` | Console cables: `{"pc": id, "device": id}` (the PC's RS-232 port to the device's Console port), and `"usb": true` on a USB cable (the PC's USB port to the device's USB console port); left out when there are none. |
| `octals` | Octal cables: `{"router": id, "line": 0-7, "device": id}` (a line of the router's octal card to the device's Console port); loading drops a cable whose router has no octal card, a line past the card's lines, a repeated line or device, and a device whose console is already cabled. Left out when there are none. |
| `next_id` | The next device id (ids are never reused). |
| `notes` | The lab's notes text. |
| `annotations` | Notes and drawings on the workspace: text, rectangle, ellipse, line, freehand, polygon, each with position and size. A drawing's `color` is its fill (a line's base colour); `outline` (false hides it), `outline_color`, `outline_width` (1-8) and `no_fill` (true) are left out while unset, and a drawing with only `color` (every file before version 4) draws as it always did: a darker shade of the colour as outline, 1 wide (3 for a line or stroke). |
| `activity` | A graded activity: `title`, `instructions` (in `language`, `en` when left out) and `translations` (language code → text), `checks` (each `label`, `points`, `type`: `config`, `config-hashed`, `ping`, `host-address`, `output`, `link` (`a`, `a_port`, `b`, `b_port`), `link-hashed`, with its fields), `hidden`, `salt`; `answer`, the answer network sealed as `scheme` (`plain`: `data` is the lab's JSON; `salt`: AES-128-CTR with an HMAC-SHA1 tag under a PBKDF2 key from the salt, which hides it from a casual look only; `password`: the same under a key from the lock password) and `data`; `initial`, the lab the student starts from (a lab file's JSON without its activity); `assessment`, the assessment tree's weights (an item's hashed key → `points` and `feedback`); `locks` (`devices`, `cables`, `config`: device ids). Lock passwords set now are kept as PBKDF2 hashes (`p2$…`). |
| `scenarios` | Simulation scenarios: `list` (each `description` and `pdus`) and `current`, the one in use. A PDU holds `num`, `source` (device id), `port`, `destination` (address or name) and `target`, `app` (`ping`, `dns`, `http`, `telnet`, `ssh`, `ftp`, `smtp`, `pop3`, `dhcp`, `tftp`, `snmp`, `arp`, `udp`, `tcp`), `simple`, `src_port`, `dst_port`, `ttl`, `tos`, `seq`, `size`, `text` (DNS name, page, file or community), `period_s` (periodic), `start_s` (seconds from when it was made) or `start_at_s` (an absolute simulated time), and what happened last: `status`, `last_s`, `fired`, `note`, `next_us`. Left out while the only scenario is empty. |

## Device

| Field | Holds |
|---|---|
| `id`, `kind`, `model`, `name`, `label`, `x`, `y` | Identity (`kind`: `router`, `switch`, `hub`, `pc`, `laptop`, `server`, `printer`, `phone`, `iot`; the Home Gateway is a `switch`, the Cloud, the modems and the Cell Tower are `hub`s, the Central Office Server is a `router`, tablets and smartphones are `laptop`s, the boards are `iot`; `model`: catalogue id such as `Switch-ML-24`, empty in old files means the kind's default), workspace label and position. |
| `ports` | The ports (below), in catalogue order. |
| `gateway`, `gateway6`, `dns`, `dhcp_client` | Hosts: default gateways, DNS server, whether the address came from DHCP. |
| `routes`, `routes6` | Static IPv4 and IPv6 routes; `distance` when one was typed (a floating static). |
| `loopbacks` | Routers and switches: loopback interfaces, sorted by number. |
| `ip_routing`, `ipv6_routing` | Multilayer switches: `ip routing`; routers: `ipv6 unicast-routing`. |
| `vlans`, `vtp`, `stp_priority`, `svis` | Switches: VLAN database (id → name), VTP mode/domain/password/version/revision/`pruning`, spanning-tree priorities per VLAN, VLAN interfaces. |
| `dhcp_pools`, `dhcp_excluded` | Routers: DHCP server pools (with `lease_secs` and `domain` when set) and excluded ranges. |
| `ospf`, `rip`, `eigrp` | Routing protocol settings (`redistribute`: each line has a `source` (`"connected"`, `"static"`, `"rip"`, `{"ospf": N}`, `{"eigrp": N}` or `{"bgp": N}`) and, when typed, `metric`, `eigrp_metric` (`bandwidth`, `delay`, `reliability`, `load`, `mtu`), `metric_type` (1 or 2) and `route_map`; `default_metric`; `distribute`: each list has a `kind` (`prefix`, `acl` or `routemap`), a `name`, `inbound` and an `iface` when it is for one interface; a route map's `sets` gains `metric_type`. An older file's `ospf.redistribute_static` and `redistribute_map` become a `redistribute` line when it is loaded, and are not written again; at most 16 redistribute lines and 32 distribute lists per process); `rip.timers` holds `timers basic` as `[update, invalid, holddown, flush]` seconds. |
| `eigrp6` | `ipv6 router eigrp N`: `asn`, `router_id`, `passive`, `shutdown`, `maximum_paths`, `variance`. |
| `eigrp_summary` | EIGRP summary addresses by interface name: `v4` (`[AS, "10.0.0.0/16"]` pairs) and `v6` (`[AS, "2001:db8::/48"]`). |
| `rip6` | RIPng: `name` (`ipv6 router rip NAME`). |
| `prefix_lists` | Routers: `ip prefix-list`: each list has a `name` and `entries` (each `seq`, `permit`, `net` such as `"10.0.0.0/8"`, and `ge` and `le` when typed). Additive: no version bump; left out when empty. Loading keeps at most 128 lists of 512 lines, drops lines with impossible lengths or sequence numbers, and cuts names to 64 characters. |
| `route_maps` | Routers: `route-map`: each map has a `name` and `clauses` (each `seq`, `permit`, `prefix_lists`, `acls` (what `match ip address` names), `metric` (`match metric`) and `sets`: `local_pref`, `metric`, `weight`, `prepend`, `next_hop`, `origin` 0 to 2). Additive; at most 128 maps of 64 clauses, 16 names per match line and 16 AS numbers in a prepend. |
| `community_lists` | Routers: `ip community-list`: each list has a `name`, `expanded` and `entries` (each `permit` and either `communities`, 32-bit numbers, or a `regex`). Additive; left out when empty. Loading drops lists with no usable line and expressions the matcher cannot use. Route-map clauses name them in `community_lists` and set communities with `sets.community` (a list; empty means `none`) and `sets.community_additive`. |
| `as_path_lists` | Routers: `ip as-path access-list N`: each list has a `name` (the number) and `entries` (each `permit` and `regex`); route-map clauses and neighbours name them in `as_path_lists`, `filter_list_in` and `filter_list_out`. Additive; left out when empty. |
| `bgp_community_new_format` | Routers: `ip bgp-community new-format`. Additive; left out when false. |
| `bgp` | Routers: `router bgp N`: `asn`, `router_id`, `log_neighbor_changes`, `default_local_pref`, `neighbors` (each `addr`, `remote_as`, `update_source`, `next_hop_self`, `shutdown`, `description`, `ebgp_multihop`, `weight`, and the policy names `prefix_list_in`, `prefix_list_out`, `route_map_in` `route_map_out`, `filter_list_in`, `filter_list_out`, `send_community`, `timers` as `[keepalive, hold]`, `route_reflector_client`, `default_originate`, `default_originate_map`, `password` (plain text, like the OSPF keys; a lab file is not a place for a real key), `remove_private_as`, `remove_private_all`, `soft_reconfiguration_in`, and `peer_group`), `networks` (prefixes), `aggregates` (each `net`, `summary_only`, `as_set`), `maximum_paths`, `maximum_paths_ibgp`, `cluster_id`, `confed_id`, `confed_peers` (member AS numbers), `always_compare_med`, `as_path_ignore`, `redistribute` (`connected`, `static`, `ospf`) and `redistribute_maps` (route-map names keyed by those words). Additive: no version bump; left out when the router has no process. Loading keeps at most 64 neighbours, 256 networks and 64 aggregates, clamps `maximum_paths` and `maximum_paths_ibgp` to 1-32, drops a `confed_id` of 0 and the zero, duplicate and own-AS entries of `confed_peers` (at most 32), ignores `remove_private_all` without `remove_private_as` and a non-zero hold time to 3 or more, drops duplicate neighbours and an AS number of 0, cuts text to 80 characters and a `password` to 25. |
| `fhrp` | VRRP and GLBP groups by interface name: `vrrp` (each `group`, `ip`, `priority`, `preempt`, `advertise_cs` (the advertisement interval in centiseconds), `track` (`[interface, decrement]` pairs), `ipv6` (the VRRPv3 virtual link-local address)), `glbp` (each `group`, `ip`, `priority`, `preempt`, `balance`), and `vrrp_v3` (`fhrp version vrrp v3`). |
| `crypto` | IPsec: ISAKMP policies and keys, transform sets, crypto maps (an entry's `ikev2_profile` makes it negotiate with IKEv2; its `lifetime` holds `seconds` and `kilobytes`), the global `sa_lifetime` (the same two fields), and `ikev2`: `proposals` (`encryption`, `integrity`, `group`), `policies` (`proposals`, `local`), `keyrings` (`peers` with `address`, `local_key`, `remote_key`) and `profiles` (`remote`, `local_id`, `auth_local`, `auth_remote`, `keyring`). Pre-shared keys are stored as typed. |
| `security` | Routers: the zone-based firewall (`zones`, `members` interface → zone, inspect `classes`, `policies`, zone `pairs`), classic inspection (`inspect_rules`, `inspect_applied` per interface with `inbound`/`outbound`), `ip_mtu` and `adjust_mss` per interface. |
| `automation` | Routers: `http`, `https`, `http_auth_local`, `restconf`, `netconf`. Hosts: the Programming app's `saved` requests (`name`, `method`, `url`, `headers`, `body`) and, on a controller, the `managed` routers (`address`, `user`, `password`, `vars`: template variables as `[name, value]` pairs). **The managed routers' passwords are stored in clear**, so a lab that holds them should be shared with care. |
| `acls`, `nat` | Access lists; NAT rules, pools and translation timeouts. |
| `access` | Passwords (enable secret is stored hashed), console and vty lines, local users, banner, SSH settings. |
| `services` | Servers: HTTP files, DNS records, DHCP pools, and the PPPoE service (`services.pppoe`: `on`, `auth` `chap` or `pap`, `pool_start`, `pool_size` up to 250, `users` up to 32 `{"name", "password"}`; left out while off and empty, and bounded on load). |
| `startup` | The saved configuration (`copy running-config startup-config`), as console lines. |
| `l2` | Switches: DHCP snooping and ARP inspection (VLANs, option 82, validation), PortFast and BPDU guard defaults, errdisable recovery causes and interval, EtherChannel load balancing, the SDM template, `mls_qos`, `loopguard_default`, `cos_dscp` (`mls qos map cos-dscp`, eight DSCP values), `span` (the monitor sessions, left out when there are none: each has an `id` 1 to 66, `ports` (full port name → `both`, `rx` or `tx`), `vlans` (VLAN → direction) and the `destination` port's name; loading keeps at most 2 sessions and 128 source ports per session, and drops what has no source and no destination); routers: `relay_trust_all` (`ip dhcp relay information trust-all`). |
| `modules` | Modular routers: the card in each bay (`"0/1": "Card-4SW"`; the cards are `Card-2S`, `Card-4S`, `Card-1GE`, `Card-2GE`, `Card-1FE`, `Card-1GF`, `Card-4SW`, `Card-8A`, the octal asynchronous card that gives no interfaces). |
| `board` | Programmable boards: `program` (the BoardScript source), `run` (it runs whenever the board has power) and `wires`, the IoT custom cables (each `pin`, such as `D0` or `A1`, and `thing`, a device id). |
| `psu` | Routers and switches: which power-supply bays hold a supply, bay 1 first (`[true, false]`); left out as from the factory, with one supply in bay 1. A device with none is switched off. |
| `adapter` | IP phones and lightweight APs: whether the power adapter is plugged in. Left out on every other device; a powered device in an older file has none and counts as plugged in. |
| `bridges` | Clouds: the port pairs joined to each other, such as `["Coaxial0", "Phone0"]`. |
| `cell` | Smartphones and tablets: `data_off` (Cellular Data switched off) and `apn` (the access point name asked for). Central office servers: `pool` (the network the devices' addresses come from; the server's own interface in it is their gateway), `apn` (the one it admits; left out admits any) and `plan_off`. Left out when default. Which tower a device is attached to, its signal and its address are worked out from the lab on load and are not saved (the address sits on the device's `Cellular0` port as `ip`, rewritten on load); a phone or tablet in an older file gets its `Cellular0` port when opened. |
| `host_fw` | PCs, laptops and servers: the Firewall app's two lists, `v4` and `v6`, each `{"on": bool, "rules": [{"allow": bool, "proto": "any"\|"tcp"\|"udp"\|"icmp", "remote"?: [address, prefix length], "ports"?: [first, last]}]}`. Left out while both lists are off and empty (every file before version 5), which means everything is let in; at most 32 rules a list, and rules that make no sense (an address of the other family, a prefix too long, ports out of order, ports on ICMP) are dropped on load. |
| `files` | Hosts: the files the Text Editor, `ftp` and `dir` share, `{"name", "content"}`. At most 32 a device, a name of 1 to 64 characters without spaces or slashes, 16 KiB of text each; longer files are cut and bad names dropped on load. |
| `pppoe_router` | Routers: PPPoE as a concentrator and as a client, `{"groups": [{"name", "template"?}], "templates": [{"number", "ip"?, "unnumbered"?: interface, "pool"?, "auth"?: "chap"\|"pap", "mtu"?, "description"?}], "dialers": [{"number", "ppp", "negotiated", "chap_hostname"?, "chap_password"?, "pap"?: {"name", "password"}, "pool"?, "mtu"?, "shutdown", "description"?, "session"?: {"port": port name, "session": as `pppoe.session`}}], "enabled": {port name: group name}, "dial_ports": {port name: pool number}}`. Left out while empty, which means no PPPoE; at most 8 groups, templates and dialers, numbers up to 255, names and secrets cut to 64 characters, an `mtu` kept between 576 and 1,492 on load. |
| `pppoe` | PCs, laptops and servers: the PPPoE Dialer's account and session, `{"username", "password", "session"?: {"server": device id, "address", "gateway", "auth": "chap"\|"pap", "id"?: PPPoE session id, "server_mac"?: the concentrator port's MAC}}`. Left out while empty (every file before version 6), which means no account and no session; a session without `id` (saved before sessions were frames) reads as id 0 and shows as lost; names and passwords are cut to 64 characters on load. |
| `netflow` | Routers: `{"version"?: 9 (5 when left out), "destinations"?: [{"addr", "port"}] (at most 2), "ingress"?: ["GigabitEthernet0/0"] (the interfaces that count flows, at most 64)}`; PCs, laptops and servers: `{"collector_port"?: 1-65535}` (the Netflow Collector listens there). Left out while empty; invalid versions, ports and names are dropped on load. The flow cache and what a collector received are dynamic state and not saved. |
| `vpn_client` | PCs, laptops and servers: the VPN Client's settings, `{"host", "group", "key", "username", "password"}` (text, each cut to 64 characters on load, left out while empty). The connection itself (its address and keys) is not saved: a lab opens disconnected. **The group key and the password are stored in clear**, so a lab that holds them should be shared with care. |
| `voice` | Routers: `telephony-service`, `ephone-dn`s and `ephone`s, option 150 per pool, and for SIP `register` (`voice register global`: `cme`, `source`, `port`, `max_dn`, `max_pool`), `reg_dns` and `pools` (`tag`, `mac`, `dn`). Phones: the TFTP server DHCP gave, `sip` (signal with SIP) and `sip_server`. PCs, laptops and servers: `softphone` (`{"server"?: call manager address, "sip"?: true}`) while the Softphone app is on; left out when it is off, and ignored on any other device. A registration and a call are not saved. |
| `environment` (lab level) | The smart things' shared room, left out when it is the default: `enabled` (the physics is on: sensors read the room), the starting `temperature` (degrees), `humidity`, `soil`, `smoke` (percent), `co` (ppm), `motion` (0 or 1), `fire` (percent flames) and `co2` (ppm, 420 when left out), and what lies outside: `outside_temperature`, `outside_humidity`, `outside_light`. The values reached while a lab runs are not saved: a loaded lab starts from these. Every value is clamped on load. |
| `environments` (lab level) | The Environments window's data, left out until something is set: `clock` (`start`: the time of day in seconds at simulated time 0, 28800 (08:00:00) by default; `frozen`: the time a paused clock shows, left out while it runs; `real_minutes` and `sim_minutes`, 1 to 1440: every `real_minutes` of simulated lab time pass `sim_minutes` in the environment) and `places`: one entry per place that sets something, `{"place": id (0 is the Intercity map), "settings": [{"id": variable id, "keys": [{"t": seconds from midnight, "v": value}]}], "notes"?, "show_notes"?, "name"? (the custom environment's name), "custom"? (variables defined by formula: each `id`, `label`, `unit`, `min`, `max`, `default`, `formula`; at most 16 a place, formulas of at most 200 characters in the language of `crates/model/src/expr.rs`), "hidden"? (the variable ids the window leaves out for this place; absent: it reads the place above; an empty list: everything shown), "interpolation"? (`step` or `smooth`; a straight line when left out)}`. One key is a constant for the day; several are keyframes joined by straight lines, wrapping around midnight. A place that sets nothing inherits from the place it stands in (closet, building, city, Intercity), then the variable's default. On load unknown places and variables are dropped, values are clamped to the variable's range, keyframes are sorted (one per time, at most 288 per variable) and a clock is brought into range. Files before version 11 have none. |
| `iot` | Smart things: `server`, `value` (a sensor's reading), `second` (a humiture sensor's humidity), `variable` (the id of the environment variable a generic environment sensor reads) and `interval` (report every N seconds of simulated time; 0 only on change); smart LEDs add `brightness` (percent, full when left out) and `colour` (`[r, g, b]`, warm white when left out) and signal generators `frequency` (Hz). Servers and home gateways: `service` and `rules`, each with `conditions` (`sensor`, `op` `>`/`<`/`=`, `value`), `any` (joined with OR, else AND), `actuator` and `on` (what the rule does when it holds; true when left out). Older rules keep `sensor` and `above` and still read. |
| `mgmt` | Routers and switches: timestamps, logging levels, NTP master, keys and trusted keys, `boot system` lines, history size, HSRP version 2 interfaces. |
| `pki` | Routers and switches: `keys` (each `label` and the two primes `p` and `q` in hex), `trustpoints` (each `name`, `enrollment` (`None`, `SelfSigned` or `{"Url": "http://A.B.C.D"}`), `subject_name`, `rsakeypair`, `revocation_none`, `certificate` and `ca_certificate` in PEM), `server` (the certificate authority: `name`, `issuer_name`, `lifetime_days`, `grant_auto`, `enabled`, `certificate`) and `generated` (keys made so far, part of the key seed). Left out when empty. Loading keeps names printable and short, primes as hex, certificates as PEM blocks within 16 KiB, at most 8 keys and 8 trustpoints; whether a prime is prime or a certificate parses is checked when it is used, so a damaged entry is unusable and never a crash. Additive: no version bump. |
| `nvram` | Routers and switches: the configuration register and files copied into flash (kept across reloads). |

## Results file

`activity.export_results` writes a results file, separate from the lab file. JSON (`format`: `netlabcraft-results`, `version` 2): `activity_id`, `title`, `student`, `time`, `elapsed_s`, `score`, `total`, `percent`, `sections`, `results` (each `label`, `section`, `points`, `passed`), `network` (the student's final lab in the lab file format above, compact and without its activity; at most 4 MiB) and `hmac` (HMAC-SHA1 over the rest, last). `activity.verify_results` runs the activity's checks against `network` and compares. Version 1 files have no `network` and are still read, without replay; newer versions are refused. CSV results (`# NetLabCraft activity results`, rows `key,value`, then `hmac,…`) carry no network and are only checked for hand edits. `time` and `elapsed_s` cannot be replayed.

## Reading rules

Readers accept older versions, refuse newer ones, cap the size (`MAX_BYTES`, 64 MiB) and
sanitize everything (duplicate ids and names, dangling or duplicate cables, non-finite positions,
missing VLAN 1, oversized lists). Dynamic state (ARP and neighbour caches, MAC tables, leases,
events) is not saved.

## Environment file

An environment can be exported from the Environments window (`environment.export`) and imported into any place (`environment.import`) as a small JSON file of its own, not a lab. It holds what one place sets, not what it inherits:

```json
{"format": "netlabcraft-environment", "version": 1, "notes": "Cold aisle", "show_notes": true,
 "variables": {"ambient_temperature": [{"t": "00:00:00", "v": 12.0}, {"t": "14:00:00", "v": 24.5}], "gravity": [{"t": "00:00:00", "v": 9.80665}]}}
```

`variables` maps a variable id (the id or its English name, `Wind speed`) to its keyframes: `t` is `HH:MM:SS` (or seconds from midnight) and `v` a number; one keyframe is a constant. Variables this version does not know are skipped, values are clamped to the variable's range, keyframes are sorted, and a file larger than 1 MB, with another `format` or a newer `version` is refused. Importing replaces what the place sets (with `merge`, only the variables the file names). The clock is part of the lab, not of this file.
